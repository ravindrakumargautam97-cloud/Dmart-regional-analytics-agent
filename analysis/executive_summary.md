# DMart Regional Director — Executive Summary

**Analysis Period:** 1 April – 29 June 2026  
**Stores Covered:** Mumbai, Pune, Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Ahmedabad  
**Data:** Synthetic training dataset, not actual DMart business records

## Executive Overview

Analysis of 450 cleaned daily store records indicates differences in revenue performance, customer footfall, target achievement and inventory availability across five stores.

Average daily revenue across all stores was **₹8,61,535.24**, with average target achievement of **91.24%**.

## Key Business Insights

**1. Mumbai — Strongest Performer**

Mumbai achieved the highest average daily revenue of ₹10,64,371.60 and average target achievement of 101.37%.

**2. Pune — Priority Improvement Area**

Pune recorded the lowest average daily revenue of ₹6,36,358.55 and average target achievement of 70.71%.

Its revenue was approximately 26.14% below the all-store average, while footfall was approximately 25.00% below the all-store average.

**3. Hyderabad — Inventory Availability Concern**

Hyderabad recorded the highest average stockout duration at 3.85 hours per day, compared with the regional average of 2.74 hours.

**4. Bengaluru — Close to Target**

Bengaluru achieved an average of 98.01% of its daily revenue target.

## Recommended Management Actions

1. **Pune:** Investigate customer footfall, sales conversion and local demand before selecting interventions.
2. **Hyderabad:** Review inventory replenishment, stockout patterns and reorder processes.
3. **Mumbai:** Identify successful operating practices that may be transferable to other stores.
4. **Regional Management:** Monitor daily revenue, target achievement, footfall and stockout hours through a consistent scorecard.

## Management Conclusion

Pune should receive priority attention for its revenue and target-achievement gap, while Hyderabad requires a focused review of stock availability. Mumbai provides a useful benchmark for further operational investigation.

These findings are descriptive and do not establish cause-and-effect relationships. Management should validate the underlying operational drivers before implementing changes.
