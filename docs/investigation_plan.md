\# DMart Regional Analytics — Investigation Plan



\## Business Problem

Investigate whether Pune's low average daily revenue can be explained by stockouts, using cleaned and validated store-level data.



\## Data Sources

\- Raw store datasets generated by the assignment-provided script

\- Cleaned datasets produced by `scripts/clean\_data.py`

\- SQLite database `dmart.db`

\- Store-level summary in `analysis/store\_summary.csv`



\## Initial Findings

\- Pune has the lowest mean daily revenue among the five stores.

\- Pune has the fourth-highest mean stockout hours.

\- Mumbai has the highest mean daily revenue.

\- Pune's lower footfall appears more consistent with its revenue gap than unusually high stockout hours.



\## Investigation Approach

1\. Verify data quality and cleaning transformations.

2\. Compare revenue, footfall and stockout hours across stores.

3\. Examine relationships between revenue and stockouts.

4\. Investigate footfall as an alternative explanation.

5\. Separate observed associations from causal conclusions.



\## Limitations

\- Correlation does not establish causation.

\- Store-level averages may hide daily variations.

\- Other factors, such as pricing, promotions, product availability and customer demand, may influence revenue.



\## Preliminary Conclusion

Current descriptive evidence does not support stockouts as the sole or primary explanation for Pune's low revenue. Lower footfall is a plausible alternative that requires further investigation.
